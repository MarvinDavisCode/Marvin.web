Where AI Can (and Can't) Help in Mental Health Triage

Triage is one of the places where the promise of AI in mental health gets tested fastest — and where the stakes of getting it wrong are highest. When someone reaches out for support, the question of "how urgent is this, and where should it go" is exactly the kind of pattern-matching problem models are good at. It's also exactly the kind of judgment call that carries real consequences if it's wrong.

Where AI genuinely helps

Used well, AI-supported triage can surface information faster, flag patterns a tired or overloaded team might miss, and reduce the time between someone asking for help and a person actually reviewing their case. It's strongest as a first pass — sorting, prioritizing, and routing — not as a final decision-maker.

Where human judgment has to stay in charge

Every triage system needs a clear line: what a model can suggest, and what a trained person must confirm. Context, risk, and nuance don't always show up in the data a model sees. Preserving human judgment at the decision point — with a clinician or trained responder able to override the system — isn't a limitation to design around; it's the design.

A practical starting point

Teams exploring this should start narrow: one well-defined triage question, a clear escalation path, and a way to audit how often the model's suggestion diverges from what a human decides. That's a far better foundation than a general-purpose system asked to do too much, too soon.
